Making a small signal audible

Low-amplitude microphone signals require amplification and filtering before headphone playback. This laboratory project combined the signal path with a parallel LED indicator, then compared circuit simulations with bench measurements.

Joint project with Xuan Li Feng, covering circuit design, SPICE simulation, breadboard assembly and oscilloscope testing.

The circuit

  • Used LM324 op-amps in two inverting amplifier stages with nominal gains of 100× and 37×.
  • Designed high-pass and low-pass filters for cut-offs of 20 Hz and 500 Hz.
  • Added a Schmitt-trigger path for an LED and an output coupling network for headphones.

Simulation and practical testing

The nominal gain-stage product was 3700× before filter attenuation. The Schmitt trigger produced the intended visual indication. Bench work also revealed excessive fixed gain, component tolerances and noisy measurements.

What I took forward

Adjustable or adaptive gain would make the circuit more tolerant of different input amplitudes. Systematic frequency-response testing would also be needed: the report did not experimentally characterise the full filter response. The outcome is a laboratory prototype, not a validated diagnostic device.
