A more wearable communication aid

GESTURA explored how augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) could fit into glasses rather than a separate handheld interface. The team combined speech-to-text, sign-recognition approaches and spoken output to support communication between signing and non-signing users.

I contributed to software architecture, machine-learning integration and interface design within the ten-person group. The overall prototype and poster represent team work, supervised by Dr Ian Radcliffe.

Two hardware iterations

The initial workflow paired an Android device with Epson BT-40 glasses. It combined microphone input, transcription, teleprompter-style text and text-to-speech. The later design moved toward Snap Spectacles and Lens Studio, bringing more of the sensing and interface into the glasses.

Recognition and interface design

  • Explored rule-based recognition using MediaPipe hand landmarks.
  • Investigated LSTM and temporal graph-convolution approaches for dynamic signs.
  • Designed speech transcription and recognised-sign output for display in an AR interface.

The poster documents the proposed pipelines and the transition from Epson BT-40 to Snap Spectacles. The project demonstrates an integrated design and prototype workflow; the supplied materials do not establish validated end-to-end recognition accuracy or latency.

Design considerations

Vocabulary coverage, noisy speech, battery life, heat and cost remain important constraints. This is an academic assistive-technology prototype, rather than a clinically validated device or a general-purpose sign-language translator.
